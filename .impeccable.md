## Design Context

### Users
Blurt serves Windows-based developers and technical users who dictate Chinese, English, and mixed-language text directly into editors, terminals, and coding assistants. The primary job is fast, dependable voice input without sending audio or text off-device; settings are visited to confirm readiness, choose audio and injection behavior, tune recognition performance, and inspect diagnostics.

### Brand Personality
Calm, precise, and private. Blurt should feel like a focused desktop instrument: technically capable, immediately legible, and quietly dependable during repeated daily use.

### Aesthetic Direction
Premium utilitarian minimalism with an editorial document character. Use a warm off-white canvas, charcoal typography, flat white surfaces, crisp one-pixel borders, and muted semantic pastels. Keep the interface compact and work-focused. Avoid gradients, glow effects, heavy shadows, oversized rounding, decorative cards, neon color, glass effects, and generic blue-purple SaaS styling.

### Design Principles
- Make current engine state and the global shortcut readable at a glance.
- Group related settings with clear hierarchy while preserving a dense desktop workflow.
- Reserve color for status and focus; rely on typography, spacing, and borders for structure.
- Keep feedback immediate and motion subtle, transform/opacity-only, and reduced-motion aware.
- Preserve transcription performance: visual polish must not add work to recording or inference paths.
